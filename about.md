I am a political science student at the University of Copenhagen, finishing a Master's degree. My research focuses on Danish electoral politics — specifically how voters form opinions, respond to elite cues, and sort across party lines.

This site documents a structured thesis workshop in which I am developing 8–12 quantitative research designs before committing to a final thesis topic. The designs are pressure-tested for originality, theoretical grounding, and empirical feasibility. My key theoretical anchors include Zaller's mainstreaming hypothesis, Petrocik's issue ownership theory, and the welfare chauvinism literature from Lefkofridi and colleagues.

## Research Interests

- Public opinion formation and elite cue-taking
- Immigration politics and welfare chauvinism
- Bloc politics and voter switching in Danish elections
- Incumbency effects in local government
- Geographic electoral polarization
- Quantitative methods in political science

## Data

All analyses use publicly available data:

- **Danish National Election Study (DNES)** — four waves: 2011, 2015, 2019, 2022
- **Chapel Hill Expert Survey (CHES)** — party position trend file
- **Danish Election Database** — polling-station-level municipal election results
- **Arbejderbevægelsens Erhvervsråd (AE)** — parish-level income data
- **Kommunernes Landsforening & Danmarks Statistik** — municipal-level administrative data
